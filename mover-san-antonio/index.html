Questions to ask a mover in San Antonio

Hiring help starts with agreeing on the work. Will you pack your own boxes? Which furniture needs to come apart? Do you want items placed in specific rooms at the destination? Ask what the proposed service includes and have any changes recorded before moving day.

Check the route your San Antonio mover will use

Walk from each large piece of furniture to the outside loading area. Look for narrow doors, tight turns, low light fixtures and steps. Measure furniture and openings when the fit looks close. A sofa that entered through a patio door may need to leave the same way.

Access deserves a separate conversation. At an apartment or condo, check elevator reservations and building requirements with management. At a house, explain driveway conditions and any restrictions on where a truck can stand. Don't assume the shortest route is available just because it appears open during your visit.

San Antonio addresses can involve very different property layouts. Give details about both homes, including stairs inside the destination, so the discussion covers unloading as well as pickup.

A preparation checklist for your mover

Finish decisions about what stays and what goes before boxes begin filling the hallway. Sorting during loading creates interruptions and makes it easier for something to travel by mistake.

Use this checklist as you prepare:

  • Label each packed box with its destination room and a brief description.
  • Keep identification, keys, medications and essential paperwork with you.
  • Empty furniture drawers unless your mover gives you different instructions.
  • Put loose furniture hardware in labeled bags and agree on where it belongs.
  • Set aside cleaning supplies and anything you'll need after the truck leaves.
  • Arrange a safe space for children and pets away from carrying paths.

Leave room names consistent between the box labels and your directions at the new home. Simple labels such as front bedroom or upstairs office reduce confusion when several rooms are empty.

Avoid last-minute problems when hiring a mover

One common mistake is leaving out belongings in sheds, attic spaces or outdoor areas when describing the job. Another is adding large pieces after the original conversation without checking how that affects the plan. Tell your mover as soon as the inventory changes.
